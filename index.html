Which road contracts deserve a closer look today?

BantAI reads every DPWH road contract each morning, scores it on six families of tested risk indicators, has an AI agent check the evidence, and puts the cases worth checking in front of a person. The person decides.

How the agentic AI works

Nine steps in four layers. Code does the counting, an AI agent checks the evidence and explains, and a person makes every decision. Each run is a GitHub Actions job; nothing is paid for and every step leaves a log.

Learning loop: decisions and outcomes (terminations, citizen reports) flow back into the weekly backtest; a rule changes only when the evidence supports it and a person approves (rule recalibration). The random grey cases tell us whether flagged cases really turn out worse than chance.

Risk indicators and how the score is derived

Six families of indicators, from bidding to construction. Every indicator has a role. Only indicators that went with worse outcomes on past contracts add to the score; the rest are shown to the reviewer as context.

Red — look first

Any integrity alert, or High priority: 2 strong signals, or 1 strong + 1 weak.

Yellow — review

Medium priority: 1 strong signal, or 2 weak signals.

Blue — check

Below Medium, but a tested watch pattern (“looks fine on paper”) or a single weak sign (e.g. an unusual price) admits it.

Grey — comparison

One random unflagged contract a day, reviewed the same way, so we can tell whether flags beat chance.

Why these flags

Each pattern was tested on thousands of past DPWH road contracts. The number is how many times higher the odds of the outcome were when the pattern was present (1× = no difference). Lines show the 95% range.

Safeguards

People decide

  • Every case is a request for a human check.
  • The AI agent reads sources and notes gaps; it never scores, decides or publishes.
  • Escalate and Close unlock only after every checklist step is done.

Evidence before rules

  • A pattern scores only after it holds up on past contracts.
  • Rule changes need a person's approval and are logged.
  • Random cases measure whether flags beat chance.

Fair to companies and people

  • Missing data never counts against anyone.
  • Name matches are marked “verify”; namesakes are common.
  • Political context never links a firm to an official without a documented source.
